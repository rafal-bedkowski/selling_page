Onboarding in standups isn't onboarding: 4 documents to write

Four documents to write in your first month on a new product team.

Rafał Będkowski 4 min read November 2026 Watch the reel
Discuss on Instagram

You joined three weeks ago. Your onboarding was a Jira invite and a Slack channel. You're still figuring out who owns what.

Not in a PM role yet? Read this as a plan for your first month.

Four short documents

  1. Product brief: start on Day 1, fill it in over your first two weeks.
  2. Decision log: keep it through your first month.
  3. Stakeholder map: write it in your first week.
  4. 30-day question list: write it before your first sprint ends.

A sprint is a short, fixed period of work, often two weeks.

When nothing is written down

I joined a team once where the previous PM had left two weeks earlier. For the first month I made product decisions based on assumptions I couldn't verify because nothing was written down. That's not a gap in documentation — that's a team one departure away from starting over.

An onboarding that happens in standups isn't onboarding. It's knowledge held by whoever is still around.

A standup is a short daily team meeting.

1. The product context brief

Write a one-page product brief on Day 1 and fill it in over your first two weeks. Mark what you know and what you're unsure about. If nothing exists, your version becomes the team's starting point.

Problem: [X]
Users: [persona]
Goal this quarter: [X]
Biggest unknown: [X]

2. The decision log

For every significant decision you encounter in your first month, write what was decided, when, and what the alternative was. This stops you reopening debates the team already had.

DecisionWhenWhyAlternative
[X][approx][one sentence][Y]

3. The stakeholder map

In your first week, list every person who can say yes or no to a product decision in your area. Write what they own and how they prefer to be involved.

A stakeholder is a person who has a say in a decision or is affected by it. For "Input": inform means you tell them, consult means you ask their opinion first, approve means they say yes or no.

Stakeholder map template
DecisionOwnerInputChannel
[type][name][inform / consult / approve][Slack / meeting]

4. The 30-day question list

Before your first sprint ends, write every question you have about the product, team, and history. Sort them into what you need this week, this month, or later. Work through the first bucket before adding to the list.

Regulated environments

In regulated environments, undocumented decisions create audit gaps. "We asked the person who left" is not an answer to a regulatory body.

Save this before your first sprint on a new team, and send it to whoever is about to hire their first PM.
